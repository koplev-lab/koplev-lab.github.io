## Vision

Cells are the basic units of life, but they do their work by organising into tissues. Fibroblasts, epithelial stem cells, lymphoid aggregates and blood vessels each shape that organisation. We study how transcription factors and alternative splicing control tissue architecture in healthy organs, and how that control changes in cancer, inflammatory bowel disease and cardiovascular disease.

Comparing cells and their tissue niches across organs and diseases, with a focus on immune-related structures, gives a broad view of human cell biology. Better models of how the genome is regulated in different cellular contexts should also help explain how the risk of complex disease is inherited.

## Our work

Single-cell and spatial omics now measure the molecular state of individual cells where they sit in the tissue. Making sense of these data is a computational problem, and machine learning is a large part of how we approach it. Doing this well takes people with different skills working on the same questions.

We take part in large open science efforts such as the Human Cell Atlas, building reference datasets and methods that others can use. In the longer term, the work points towards earlier detection of cancer and inflammatory disease from liquid biopsies and clinical imaging, and towards new ways to modulate the immune system as therapy.
