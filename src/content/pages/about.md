## Vision

Cells are the basic units of life, but they do their work by organising into tissues. Fibroblasts, epithelial stem cells, lymphoid aggregates and blood vessels each shape that organisation. We study how transcription factors and alternative splicing control tissue architecture in healthy organs, and how that control changes in cancer, inflammatory bowel disease and cardiovascular disease.

Comparing cells and their tissue niches across organs and diseases, with a focus on immune-related structures, gives a broad view of human cell biology. Better models of how the genome is regulated in different cellular contexts should also help explain how the risk of complex disease is inherited.

## Methods and models

Single-cell and spatial omics now measure the molecular state of individual cells where they sit in the tissue. Turning those measurements into an understanding of how tissues are built is a modelling problem, and we develop computational and statistical methods for it rather than only applying existing ones.

Earlier work includes a Bayesian dose-response framework that finds drug combinations whose effect depends on the order in which the drugs are given, and gene regulatory network models that combine genetics, clinical data and RNA sequencing from seven tissues to explain much of the inherited risk of coronary artery disease. Current work uses deep learning to read the effect of DNA variants in individual cell types, and machine learning, including AI agents that run analyses from start to finish, to find cell states and tissue niches in spatial data.

## People from many disciplines

The questions are biological, but many of the methods come from machine learning, statistics, mathematics and computer science. We welcome people trained in those fields as much as people from biology and medicine, and we learn a great deal from each other's backgrounds.

We take part in large open science efforts such as the Human Cell Atlas, building reference datasets and methods that others can use. In the longer term, the work points towards earlier detection of cancer and inflammatory disease from liquid biopsies and clinical imaging, and towards new ways to modulate the immune system as therapy.
